Early-stage SaaS teams often ask whether to spend scarce cash on ads or to trade ad space with other companies. The two solve different problems, so the better question is what you need right now: speed and control, or reach without a budget.

## What each one is

**Paid ads** buy visitors. You choose the targeting, set a budget, and pay for impressions or clicks on a platform such as a search engine or a social network.

**An ad swap** trades visibility. You show another company's ad to your visitors, and they show yours to theirs. No cash changes hands. Both sides pay with ad space and attention.

## How they compare

### Cost
- **Paid ads:** cash, usually spent before you know whether your message converts.
- **Ad swap:** no cash, but you give up ad space and spend time on setup and tracking.

### Speed
- **Paid ads:** you can start the same day and stop the same day.
- **Ad swap:** depends on finding partners, or on how fast a network can match you.

### Control
- **Paid ads:** you choose the targeting, the message and the budget.
- **Ad swap:** you control your own creative and placement, but not your partner's traffic.

### Audience quality
- **Paid ads:** only as good as your targeting.
- **Ad swap:** you reach the existing visitors of a related product, which can be a warmer context, provided the partner's audience genuinely resembles yours.

### Scale
- **Paid ads:** grows with budget, until the cost per signup starts to rise.
- **Ad swap:** grows with the number and traffic of your partners, and is limited by what they can show.

### Main risk
- **Paid ads:** burning budget on clicks that never sign up.
- **Ad swap:** a poor fit, or a partner with little traffic, giving you little in return.

Both need measurement. Without per-campaign or per-partner tracking you are guessing.

## When paid ads make sense

- You have a budget you can afford to lose while you learn.
- You need results quickly, for example around a launch.
- You already know which message and audience convert, and you want to scale them.

## When an ad swap makes sense

- Your cash is close to zero, but your site has real visitors to offer in return.
- You can name complementary products whose users would plausibly want yours.
- You are happy to wait for results and learn from small tests.

## Rules for any swap

1. **Keep competitors out.** Showing a rival to your visitors helps the rival.
2. **Check real traffic.** Look at the page where your ad would appear, not only the site's total.
3. **Agree the placement and the period.** A footer link for a month is not the same as a visible slot.
4. **Approve what appears on your site.** Your visitors trust you, so review the ads they will see.
5. **Keep it balanced.** Give about as much exposure as you get, or know exactly why you are not.
6. **Track every partner separately.** Judge results on signups, not on clicks.

## How to decide

If you have no budget, start with swaps and small tests. If you have budget and need speed, test paid ads with a small, capped amount. Many teams do both: paid ads to learn which message works, swaps to stretch the reach once it does.

Whichever you choose, set a simple success measure before you start, such as signups per partner or cost per signup, and review it after a month.
